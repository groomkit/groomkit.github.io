XGen Hair Shaders

Assigning and tweaking hair materials in Maya's XGen can be a tedious process involving the Hypershade and hunting down specific shading nodes. GroomKit's Shaders module acts as an instant material library and procedural variation engine for fast viewport look-dev.

GroomKit Hair Shaders.

Applying Hair Shader Presets

GroomKit ships with several built-in realtime hair shader presets, while also offering full support for creating your own custom palettes.

  • Color Swatches: Previews the Root, Tip, and Primary Highlight colors of the preset. Clicking on them focuses the Attribute Editor.
  • Apply Button: Applies the shader preset to the active XGen description.
  • Var Button: Applies procedural clump-based color variation on top of the shader preset.
  • Double-Click: Cascades the shader preset or variation to all descriptions in the current collection.

Shader presets are highly configurable and highly reusable between projects. Users can either use the built-in library or create new custom shaders that can be applied with just a single click.

Customization

While the built-in presets provided by GroomKit serve simply as a starting point, the true power of the Shader module lies in its flexibility. Users are encouraged to create their own custom shader presets to aid in look-dev during the grooming process.

Shader Preset Breakdown

A preset row is a quick preview of the hair material to be applied to your XGen description. It provides immediate functionality through buttons and color swatches.

  • Preset Name: The name of the hair shader preset (either built-in or user-generated).
  • Color Swatches: A brief preview of the root, tip, and highlight colors. Clicking any of these will open and focus the connected hair shader material in the Attribute Editor for customization.
  • Apply Button: Applies the preset to the currently active XGen description.
  • Var Button: Adds a procedural expression on top of the shader to produce clump-based color variation.
  • (Double Click): Double-clicking either Apply or Var will execute the action across all descriptions in the currently active XGen collection.
  • Right Mouse Click: Right-clicking the preset name allows you to restore a built-in preset to its factory default, or delete a user-created preset entirely.


Procedural Hair Color Variation (Var)

Perfectly uniform hair color can look synthetic. Real hair has micro-variations in lightness and darkness on a per-strand basis. The Var (Variation) button injects a complex procedural noise expression into XGen's GLRenderer, giving you viewport sliders to control the chaos.

Applying clump-based color variation.

Once applied, you can adjust these properties in the XGen Editor Window under the Preview/Output > Primitive Color tab:

  • Freq (Frequency): Controls the scale and density of the variation noise. Lower values create larger, broader patches of grouped colors. Higher values generate finer, more chaotic, strand-by-strand detail.
  • Seed: Changes the random starting point of the noise pattern. Adjust this to quickly shuffle the variation layout without changing the scale.
  • Min: Defines the darkest lightness value/multiplier of the color variation.
  • Max: Defines the brightest lightness value/multiplier of the color variation.

(Note: To clear the variation and return to a flat color, right-click the Var button and select "Clear", or simply re-click the Apply button.)


How to Create Custom Hair Shaders

Advanced Customization

The provided shaders might not always give the exact look you're looking for. Because of this, we encourage you to experiment with the shader parameters to match your vision and understand how they work.

Hair shaders in GroomKit are grouped into two categories:

  • hairPhysicalShader: Maya's standard viewport-friendly shader that comes natively with XGen descriptions.
  • aiStandardHair: Arnold's realism-based shader featuring advanced highlight, transmission, and melanin controls.

Customizing: Apply a preset, then click any of its Color Swatches to instantly focus the Attribute Editor on that exact active shader node.

Customize Maya hairPhysicalShader

Click to expand configuration parameters...

The hairPhysicalShader is great for fast viewport look-dev and stylized grooms.

  • Root / Tip Color: Drives the primary base color gradient of the hair strand.
  • Diffuse Weight: Controls how strongly the Root and Tip colors are displayed. A value of 0 means the hair is entirely driven by highlights.
  • Primary / Secondary Highlight Color: Drives the color and tint of the hair's gloss and shine.
  • Other Parameters: Attributes like Scatter, Shift, and Transmission offer subtle stylistic changes to the visual effect and can generally be left at default for standard looks.

Customize Arnold aiStandardHair

Click to expand configuration parameters...

The aiStandardHair is Arnold's physically accurate hair model, relying on melanin rather than simple diffuse colors.

  • Melanin & Melanin Redness: The primary driver for realistic hair. 0.0 yields blonde/white hair, while 1.0 yields black hair. Melanin Redness adds auburn/ginger tones.
  • Roughness: Controls the sharpness of the specular highlight. Lower values create shiny/glossy hair, while higher values create dry/matte hair.
  • Specular Tints: Allows you to tint the natural white highlights with specific colors (great for faking colored lighting or dyed reflections).
  • Diffuse Color & Weight: Used specifically for dyed, painted, or stylized hair. Increasing the diffuse weight overrides the natural Melanin calculation with the chosen Diffuse Color.

Saving Custom Shader Presets

Once you have tweaked a shader in the Attribute Editor to your liking, you can save it to GroomKit for future use. There are two ways to do this:

1. Automated Method (Override existing preset)

If you are tweaking a preset that is already applied from the GroomKit UI, GroomKit tracks it.

Simply click the Apply button on that same preset row again. GroomKit will detect the changes and automatically overwrite the preset file with your new settings.

2. Manual Method (Create new preset)

If you want to save your tweaks as an entirely new preset without overwriting an old one:

  1. Ensure the Attribute Editor is focused on your active hairPhysicalShader or aiStandardHair.
  2. Click the Presets* button at the top right of the Attribute Editor.
  3. Select Save [Node] Preset... and give it a name.
  4. In the GroomKit Shaders UI, click the large Reload all Hair Shader Presets button.

A new material will instantly appear in the User Presets section!

Restoring Shader Presets

Right-click the name of any built-in shader preset and select Reset to restore its colors and parameters back to factory defaults. Right-clicking a User Preset will allow you to Delete it.
